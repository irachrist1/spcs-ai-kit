# Lesson 2: A clean inbox

**What he has at the end.** An inbox that holds only what needs him, replies drafted for those, and a skill that makes "clean my inbox" work the same way every day.

**Time.** About 30 minutes. Most of it is you working while he watches.

**New idea.** A skill.

**Needs.** Lesson 1.

## Steps

### 1. Say what's about to happen

**Say.** In two lines. You'll read the last 30 days of his inbox and sort it into three piles. Nothing gets moved until he's seen the plan.

**Do.** Read the last 30 days. If that's too much to read well, start with 7 days and widen. Sort every thread into one of three piles.

- **Needs him.** A person waiting on a reply or a decision.
- **Routine.** Receipts, notifications, newsletters he reads, things already handled.
- **Junk.** Mail he should never see again. Cold sales, daily promos, services he doesn't use.

**Say.** The three counts, and the two or three senders behind most of the routine and junk. Four lines at most. Ask if he wants to see the "needs him" list.

**Wait.** His yes.

### 2. Show what needs him

**Say.** The "needs him" threads, one line each, most urgent first. If there are more than seven, show seven and say how many more there are.

**Wait.** His reaction. He may say some don't need a reply. Take those out.

### 3. His yes, the clean-up plan

**Say.** The plan in three lines.

- Archive the routine and junk piles. Explain archiving in one line. It's a filing cabinet, out of sight and still searchable, and it can be undone. You never delete.
- Draft a reply for each thread that needs him. Drafts only.
- Blocking the junk senders for good comes in Lesson 3, because it needs his browser.

Ask if there's a sender he wants left alone, and whether to go ahead.

**Wait.** His yes. He can drop any part.

### 4. Archive

**Do.** Archive the approved threads. Then read the inbox again and confirm what's left.

**Say.** One line with the before and after. "Your inbox went from 412 threads to 9."

**Wait.** Nothing. Move to the drafts in the same message.

### 5. Draft the replies, starting with one

**Do.** Draft the most urgent reply and save it as a draft on its thread.

**Say.** Ask him to open it in Drafts and tell you what he'd change. Listen for his voice. Is he shorter than you wrote, warmer, more direct?

**Wait.** His edits. Apply them, and note his preferences in the progress file.

### 6. Draft the rest

**Do.** Draft the others the same way, using what you learned from his edits.

**Say.** A short list. Who each draft is to, and its point in a few words. He reads and sends them himself.

**Wait.** His go-ahead to continue.

### 7. Save it as a skill

**Say.** Explain *a skill*. Tell him you'll save how he likes his inbox cleaned, so the three words "clean my inbox" do all of this next time.

**Do.** Create a personal skill at `~/.claude/skills/clean-inbox/SKILL.md`. Keep it short and write it from what happened today.

```
---
name: clean-inbox
description: Cleans the inbox the way its owner likes it. Use when he says "clean my inbox" or asks what needs him in his email.
---

1. Read everything in the inbox since the last clean.
2. Sort into needs him, routine and junk. (His own examples of each go here.)
3. Archive routine and junk. Never delete.
4. Draft a reply for each thread that needs him. Never send.
5. Report in five lines or fewer. What needs him, most urgent first, and how many drafts are waiting.

How he writes. (His voice, from his edits.)
Senders to leave alone. (His list.)
```

**Say.** Read him the skill in three or four plain lines, so he knows what it holds. Ask if anything's missing.

**Wait.** His answer. Adjust the skill.

### 8. Wrap up

**Do.** Save the junk senders in the progress file for Lesson 3. Update the rest.

**Say.** Three short lines.

- What he has. A clean inbox, the drafts waiting, and a skill.
- What he learned. He approves a plan and the agent does the work. A skill means he never explains a job twice.
- What to try. Tomorrow morning, open the Code tab, choose `spcs-ai` and say "clean my inbox". It takes two minutes.

Then offer Lesson 3, or a stop.

## If something goes wrong

- **You can't archive through the connector.** Tell him plainly, keep the list, and do it in Lesson 3 through the browser. Carry on with the drafts.
- **He uses Outlook.** Same steps. Archive works the same way.
- **The new skill doesn't respond to "clean my inbox".** It loads in a new chat. Tell him it will work from the next chat on.
- **He's nervous about archiving.** Do one sender first and show him where the mail went (All Mail in Gmail, Archive in Outlook). Then ask again.
