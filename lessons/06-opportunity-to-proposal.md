# Lesson 6: From opportunity to proposal

**What he has at the end.** A short list of open opportunities for SPCS with a go or skip call on each, and a full proposal drafted for the best one, saved in his Drive for him to decide.

**Time.** About an hour. Most of it is you working while he does something else. Tell him so.

**New ideas.** None. He uses everything from the earlier lessons.

**Needs.** Lesson 1 for Drive. Lesson 3 helps, because some portals only work in his browser.

## Steps

### 1. Say what's about to happen

**Say.** Three lines. Finding tenders, reading 60-page documents and writing a first draft used to take days. You do the hunting and the drafting. He makes the call and adds what only he knows. Ask one question. Is there a kind of work he most wants more of right now?

**Wait.** His answer.

### 2. Learn what SPCS sells

**Do.** Read https://spcstech.com and its Technology and Build pages. Search his Drive for a company profile, past proposals and case studies. Write down, for yourself, what each side of SPCS does and what proof exists for it.

**Say.** One line on what you found. If something big is missing, like any record of past work, ask where it lives. One question only.

**Wait.** Only if you asked.

### 3. Hunt

**Say.** Tell him this takes about fifteen minutes and he can step away.

**Do.** Search for opportunities that are open today. Look in all of these.

- **Public tenders.** Rwanda's e-procurement portal Umucyo (https://www.umucyo.gov.rw/), and tenders and expressions of interest from RAIA, the Rwanda AI Agency.
- **Development partners.** Procurement pages of the World Bank, GIZ, UNDP, the African Development Bank and similar programmes active in Rwanda and East Africa.
- **Companies with a reason to buy.** Ones that just raised money, announced a digital project or are visibly struggling with AI (SPCS Technology). Offices, hotels, restaurants, schools and homes being built or renovated (SPCS Build, and often Technology too).
- **Channels he follows.** If he's in WhatsApp groups or newsletters that share opportunities, ask him to paste in this week's messages. Treat them as leads to check, not facts.

Drop anything closed, anything you can't source, and anything SPCS can't deliver.

### 4. Report the best five

**Say.** Best first. For each one, who it's from, the deadline, which side of SPCS it fits, the effort, and your call (go or skip) with the reason in a line or two. Link the source. Be honest about fit. A skip with a good reason is worth more than a weak go. This is his own result, so a table is fine here.

**Wait.** His reaction.

### 5. His yes, pick one

**Say.** Ask which one to write up. If he has no view, recommend one and say why.

**Wait.** His pick.

### 6. Read the full documents

**Do.** Download the tender or brief in full and read all of it. Work out what they want delivered and by when, who can bid, what the bid must contain, how it's scored, and what SPCS is missing.

**Say.** Five lines at most. The deadline, the two or three requirements that matter most, and each gap with how to close it (a partner, a document to request, a reference to ask for). Ask if he still wants to go for it.

**Wait.** His yes.

### 7. Draft the proposal

**Say.** Tell him this takes about twenty minutes.

**Do.** Write a complete proposal, not an outline. Use this shape unless the tender sets its own format, which always wins.

1. **The problem, in their words.** Show you read their brief.
2. **What we'll deliver.** Specific outcomes and dates, not activities.
3. **How we'll do it.** Phases, each with what they get at the end of it.
4. **Why SPCS.** Past work that happened, and nothing else. Framing it well is fine. If a reference is weak, say so to him, not to the client.
5. **Team.** Roles, with a blank for each name he must confirm.
6. **Price and terms.** Leave the numbers to him. Lay out the structure (phases, what each costs, payment terms) so he only fills in figures.
7. **Next step.** One clear ask.

Write it the way START.md says. Confident and specific. "We'll have your team using it within six weeks", never "We believe we could potentially help".

Save it as a Google Doc in his Drive, or a Word file in his Documents, in a folder called "SPCS proposals".

### 8. Check it, then hand it over

**Do.** Reread the tender's requirements and tick each one against the draft.

**Say.** Where the document is, and a short list in the chat (not in the document) of what's missing, every blank he must fill, and the deadline. Ask him to read the first page and tell you what he'd change.

**Wait.** His edits. Apply them.

### 9. Wrap up

**Do.** Update the progress file. Save the lesson's output to his repo if Lesson 4 is done.

**Say.** Three short lines.

- What he has. Five checked opportunities and one proposal draft.
- What he learned. The agent hunts and drafts. He decides and adds what only he knows.
- The date to remember. The deadline.

Nothing gets submitted or sent without his yes. Offer to save this as a skill, so "find me opportunities" works the same way next week. Then offer Lesson 7, or a stop.

## If something goes wrong

- **A portal needs a login.** Ask him to sign in in his browser, then carry on. Never ask for a password in the chat.
- **Nothing good this week.** Say so plainly, show the best near misses, and say where you'd look next. Don't pad the list.
- **The tender is a scanned PDF.** Read it page by page as images. Tell him about anything you couldn't read.
- **There's no record of past work.** Ask him to tell you about two or three projects in his own words, and write them up as short case studies first. Save them in the proposals folder.
