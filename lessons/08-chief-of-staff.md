# Lesson 8: Your chief of staff

**What he has at the end.** An agent with a written brief about him, which thinks ahead and tells him what he's missing. And a clear view of when a second agent is worth adding.

**Time.** About 45 minutes for the brief. The rest proves itself over the following week.

**New ideas.** None.

**Needs.** Lessons 1, 2 and 7, and a week or two of using them. A chief of staff built on habits he doesn't have yet is noise.

## Steps

### 1. Say what changes

**Say.** Three lines. So far he asks and you do. A chief of staff doesn't wait to be asked. It watches his inbox, calendar and deadlines, acts on what's safe, and comes to him with decisions. What makes that work is a brief, a letter from him to you about who he is and what he wants watched.

**Wait.** His go-ahead.

### 2. Get the chief of staff kit

**Do.** Tonny's public kit is at https://github.com/irachrist1/chief-of-staff-kit. Download it into `spcs-ai/chief-of-staff-kit` the same way you download this kit, and read all of it. Follow its `START.md`, with one change. It tells you to interview him in one message. Don't. Ask its questions one or two at a time, over several messages, the way this kit teaches. Skip any question the progress file already answers.

### 3. Interview him

**Say.** One or two questions per message. Start with his few big goals right now, then the admin he avoids, then the opportunities he wants hunted, then anything you must never do without asking.

**Wait.** After each. Reflect back what you heard before the next one.

### 4. Write the brief and show it

**Do.** Fill in the kit's `brief-template.md` in his voice. Delete the optional sections that don't apply to him. Save it as `spcs-ai/brief.md`.

**Say.** Don't paste the whole brief. Give him the five lines that matter most and where the file is. Ask him to read the opening section and tell you what's wrong.

**Wait.** His edits. Apply them.

### 5. His yes, the routines

**Say.** The brief asks for a few routines. Propose them one line each. A morning brief (he has it already), a night wrap, a weekly review, and one sweep every couple of hours while he's working. Say which you'd start with. Ask which he wants.

**Wait.** His picks. Set up only those. Fewer is better.

### 6. Check what works

**Do.** Run the kit's `setup-check.md` on yourself.

**Say.** Not the full table. Three lines. What's working today, what needs one more connection from him, and the first thing you'll do without being asked.

**Wait.** His reaction.

### 7. Wrap up

**Do.** Update the progress file. Save everything to his repo.

**Say.** Three short lines.

- What he has. A brief, the routines he picked, and an agent that starts work without him.
- What he learned. The brief is the job. When the agent drifts, he tells it to reread the brief.
- What to expect. "Partly" on day one. It proves itself over a week.

## A second agent, when he asks

One agent is enough for a long time. More agents means more messages to read, and an agent he doesn't act on is noise. Suggest a second one only when he hits one of these.

- He wants work to keep happening while his computer is off.
- He wants an agent he can reach from his phone's messenger.
- He wants a second opinion on something big, from a different company's model.

These are the ones Tonny uses, as of November 2026. They change every few weeks, so check each one's site and whether it works in Rwanda before he signs up. Suggest one, with the reason that fits him. Never the whole table.

| Agent | Good for | How Tonny uses it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ChatGPT and Codex** | A second opinion from a different company, and voice conversations on the go. | A second brain for decisions he cares about. |
| **Grok Bot** (xAI) | Has its own email address, so he and other agents can email it work. It can sign up for services and contact businesses. | It leads his nightly review and moves anything unfinished to a new slot. |
| **Wabi** | A messenger app with an agent in every chat. It builds small apps on the spot and asks before important actions. | It sends him the most useful messages of all his agents. |
| **Hark** | A personal assistant built around privacy. It watches email and calendar and can work through websites. | It watches his personal inbox and passes on what's important. |
| **Muse** (Meta) | Runs errands on its own, in its own app and in WhatsApp. | It launched in the US first. |

Give every agent the same brief. They never split the work into lanes. Each one owns all of it, they share findings in one place so nothing reaches a person twice, and only he gives instructions. The chief of staff kit's team room sets this up.

## If something goes wrong

- **He hasn't used the earlier lessons much.** Say so kindly, and suggest a week with the morning routine first.
- **The brief feels too long to him.** It's written for you, not for him. He only needs to agree with the opening section and the "never do" list.
- **A routine fires too often.** Turn it down or off. The brief's rule is one sweep, not a separate job for each thing.
