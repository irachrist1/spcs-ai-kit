# Lesson 3: From opportunity to proposal

**What you'll have at the end:** a short list of real, open opportunities for SPCS, a go or skip call on each, and a full proposal drafted for the best one, saved in your Drive and ready for you to decide.

**Time:** about an hour. Most of it is the agent working while you do something else.

## Why this lesson

This is where agents pay for themselves. Finding tenders, reading 60-page documents and writing a first draft used to take days. An agent does the hunting and the drafting. You make the call and add what only you know.

## Steps (for the agent)

### 1. Know what SPCS sells

Read https://spcstech.com, the Technology and Build pages, and anything your person shares about past work (a company profile, past proposals, case studies in their Drive). Write down, for yourself, what each side of SPCS does and what proof exists for it. Ask your person one question only if something big is missing.

### 2. Hunt

Search for opportunities that are open today. Look in all of these, not just one:

- **Public tenders.** Rwanda's e-procurement portal Umucyo (https://www.umucyo.gov.rw/), and tenders and expressions of interest (REOIs) from RAIA, the Rwanda AI Agency.
- **Development partners.** Procurement pages of the World Bank, GIZ, UNDP, the African Development Bank and similar programmes active in Rwanda and East Africa.
- **Companies with a reason to buy.** Ones that just raised money, announced a digital project, or are visibly struggling with AI (SPCS Technology). Offices, hotels, restaurants, schools and homes being built or renovated (SPCS Build, and often Technology too).
- **Channels your person follows.** If they're in WhatsApp groups or newsletters that share opportunities, ask them to paste in this week's messages. Treat them as leads to check, not facts.

Drop anything closed, anything you can't source, and anything SPCS clearly can't deliver.

### 3. Report the best five

One table, best first:

| Opportunity | Who | Deadline | Side (Technology or Build) | Effort | Call |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

**Call** is go or skip, with the reason in two lines. Link the source for each. Be honest about fit. A skip with a good reason is worth more than a weak go.

### 4. Your yes: pick one

Ask which one to write up. If they don't have a view, recommend one and say why.

### 5. Read the full documents

Download the tender or brief in full and read all of it. List:

- What they want delivered, and by when.
- Who can bid (registration, tax clearance, years in business, licences).
- What the bid must contain (forms, references, CVs, a financial offer).
- How it's scored.
- What SPCS is missing, and how to close each gap (a partner, a document to request, a reference to ask for).

### 6. Draft the proposal

Write a complete proposal, not an outline. Shape it like this, unless the tender sets its own format, which always wins:

1. **The problem, in their words.** Show you read their brief.
2. **What we'll deliver.** Specific outcomes and dates, not activities.
3. **How we'll do it.** Phases, each with what they get at the end of it.
4. **Why SPCS.** Real past work only. Framing it well is fine. Claiming work that didn't happen is never fine. If a reference is weak, say so to your person, not to the client.
5. **Team.** Roles, with a placeholder for each name your person must confirm.
6. **Price and terms.** Leave the numbers to your person. Lay out the structure (phases, what each costs, payment terms) so they only fill in figures.
7. **Next step.** One clear ask.

Write it the way START.md says. Confident and specific: "We'll have your team using it within six weeks", never "We believe we could potentially help". No filler, no em dashes.

Save it as a Google Doc in their Drive (or a Word file in their documents folder), in a folder called "SPCS proposals". Open it for them.

### 7. Check it before they see it

Reread the tender's requirements and tick each one against the draft. List anything missing, every placeholder they must fill, and the deadline, at the top of your message (not in the document).

### 8. Wrap up

Three short lines: what they now have (five checked opportunities and one proposal draft), what they learned (the agent hunts and drafts, they decide and add what only they know), and the deadline that matters.

Note the result in the progress file. Nothing gets submitted or sent without their yes.

## If something goes wrong

- **Umucyo or another portal needs a login:** ask your person to sign in in the browser, then carry on. Never ask for their password in the chat.
- **Nothing good this week:** say so plainly, show the best near misses, and suggest where to look next. Don't pad the list.
- **The tender is a scanned PDF:** read it page by page as images. Note anything you couldn't read.
