# The ideas, in plain words

Eleven ideas come up in the lessons. Each one is here with an everyday comparison and the reason it matters to you.

For the agent. Explain an idea the first time it comes up, one idea per message, in your own words. Tie it to what just happened on his screen. Never read out two in a row.

## An agent

A chatbot answers questions. An agent does the work.

Chat is like phoning a smart friend for advice. You still do everything yourself afterwards. An agent is like a capable new assistant sitting at your desk. You hand over a job, it opens your mail, your files and your browser, and it comes back with the job done for you to check.

**Why it matters to you.** You stop copying things in and out of a chat window. You say what you want and review the result.

Comes up in Lesson 1.

## The Code tab

The Claude app has three tabs. In **Chat**, Claude can only talk. In **Code**, Claude can work on your computer. Cowork is a third mode this kit doesn't use.

Chat is an adviser on the phone. Code is the same adviser in the room with you, hands on the keyboard. The name is misleading. You won't write any code. It's called Code because programmers used it first.

**Why it matters to you.** Everything in this kit happens in the Code tab. If Claude ever says it can't open or install something, check you're in Code.

Comes up in Lesson 1.

## A command

A command is a typed instruction to the computer, in place of clicking. "List every app on this computer" is one short line.

It's like telling a driver the address in place of steering there yourself. Claude types the commands. Before each one runs, a box shows you the line and asks you to allow it. That's you countersigning.

**Why it matters to you.** Commands are how Claude does in seconds what takes you twenty clicks. The box is how you stay in charge. For looking and reading, choosing "always allow" is fine. Claude tells you when one deserves a careful read.

Comes up in Lesson 1.

## A connector

A connector is a door between Claude and one of your apps, like Gmail or your calendar. You open it by signing in once. You can close it any time from the same screen.

Think of a key card you give a new assistant. It opens one room, you can see which rooms they have, and you can take it back.

**Why it matters to you.** An agent is only as useful as what it can reach. Without the Gmail connector, Claude can talk about email. With it, Claude can read yours and write replies.

Comes up in Lesson 1.

## A draft and a send

A draft sits in your Drafts folder and nobody else sees it. A send leaves, and you can't take it back.

A draft is a letter on your desk. A send is a letter in the post. Claude writes drafts freely. It sends only when you say yes to that exact message.

**Why it matters to you.** This is why you can let an agent work fast in your inbox. Nothing goes out under your name until you've read it.

Comes up in Lesson 1.

## A chat and its notes

Every new chat starts with a blank memory, like a new shift worker who wasn't there yesterday. So Claude keeps a handover note, a small file called `progress.md` in your `spcs-ai` folder, and reads it at the start of every chat.

**Why it matters to you.** To carry on, open the Code tab, choose the `spcs-ai` folder and say "let's continue". You never have to explain yourself twice.

Comes up in Lesson 1.

## A skill

A skill is a saved instruction sheet for a job you do again and again. It holds how you like the job done.

It's the written procedure you give a new hire so you never explain the job twice. Once "clean my inbox" is a skill, those three words get the same careful job every time, your way.

**Why it matters to you.** Without skills, you re-explain. With them, your agent gets better at your work every week.

Comes up in Lesson 2.

## Claude in Chrome

Claude in Chrome is a small add-on for the Chrome browser. It lets Claude use your browser the way you do. It clicks, types and reads pages, on sites where you're already signed in, while you watch.

It's like handing the mouse to a colleague sitting next to you. A connector is a private side door built for agents, so it's quicker. Claude in Chrome is for every site that has no such door.

**Why it matters to you.** Forms, portals, settings pages and sign-ups stop being your job. When a page asks for a password or a "prove you're human" puzzle, Claude stops and hands it to you.

Comes up in Lesson 3.

## GitHub and a repo

A repo is a folder that remembers every change ever made to it. GitHub is the website where repos are kept.

Picture a Drive folder with a perfect memory. You can see what changed, when, and go back to any earlier day. GitHub is the bank vault that folder sits in.

**Why it matters to you.** Your work with Claude gets backed up and can always be undone. It's also how I share kits like this one with you.

Comes up in Lesson 4.

## A key

A key is a password made for a program in place of a person. Its full name is an API key.

It works like a key card for one door. Our sending key can do one thing, send email from spcstech.com. Anyone holding it can do that too, which is why it's handled with care.

**Why it matters to you.** A key never goes into a chat, a document or an email. It lives in 1Password, and Claude reads it from there without showing it on screen.

Comes up in Lesson 5.

## A routine

A routine is a job on a timer. Claude starts it by itself at the time you set, like every weekday at 7.

It's a standing order at the bank. You set it up once and it happens without you. The Claude app may call it a scheduled task.

**Why it matters to you.** The work is done before you sit down. Your inbox is already sorted and the brief is waiting.

Comes up in Lesson 7.
