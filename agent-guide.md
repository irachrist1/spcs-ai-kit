# Agent guide

Which agents to install, what each is good at, and when one is enough. This reflects what I actually use, as of November 2026. These products change every few weeks, so your agent should check each one's current site before you sign up.

## Start with one

**One agent is enough for your first two weeks.** Use Claude for everything until you hit a job it can't do. More agents means more messages to read, and an agent you don't act on is just noise. I learned that the hard way. Mine sent me plenty of useful messages and I acted on very few of them.

Add a second agent only for one of these reasons:

- You want work to keep happening while your computer is off.
- You want an agent you can reach from your phone's messenger.
- You want a second opinion on something that matters, from a different company's model.

## The core

| Agent | What it's good at | Install |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Claude** (desktop app) | Your main agent. It works on your computer, reads and writes your files, connects to Gmail, Calendar, Drive and Notion, builds websites and documents, and runs Blender for 3D. This kit runs in it. | https://claude.ai/download |
| **ChatGPT and Codex** | A second brain from a different company. Good for a second opinion, voice conversations on the go, and Codex for code. | https://chatgpt.com |

Inside the Claude desktop app, the **Code** tab is where the real work happens, because it can run things on your computer. The chat tab is for quick questions.

## Agents that work on their own

These live in their own inbox or messenger and keep working without your computer. Each one needs the same written brief, or they drift. The chief of staff kit at the end of this page writes it for you.

| Agent | What it's good at | How I use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Grok Bot** (xAI) | Has its own email address, so you and other agents can email it work. It can sign up for services and contact businesses. | Mine is called B. It leads my nightly review, where it moves anything I didn't finish to a new slot. |
| **Wabi** | A messenger app with an agent in every chat. It builds small apps on the spot and asks before important actions. iPhone, Android and Mac. | It sends me the most useful messages of all my agents. |
| **Hark** | A personal assistant built around privacy. It watches email and calendar and can work through websites. Web, iPhone and Android. | It watches my personal inbox and passes on what matters. |
| **Muse** (Meta) | Runs errands on its own. It books travel, buys things, sends email and keeps going after you close the app. Works in its own app and in WhatsApp. | It launched in the US first. Check whether it works in Rwanda before you count on it. |

| Tool | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| **1Password** | Where shared SPCS keys and logins reach you. Agents can read from it without you pasting secrets into a chat. |
| **Granola** | Takes meeting notes on its own. Most of what you promise people lives there, so agents check it first. |

## How a team of agents works

Once you run more than one, run them like a company. You're the CEO. One agent (Claude, for me) is the chief of staff.

- **Every agent owns everything in the brief.** Never split the work into lanes. If two agents both catch something, good.
- **They do the work, not recommend it.** If it's safe and reversible, they act and tell you after. Only decisions come to you.
- **One shared room.** They post findings to one email thread a day, so nothing goes to a real person twice.
- **Only you give instructions.** What one agent writes to another is information, not an order.

The setup for this lives in my public chief of staff kit, https://github.com/irachrist1/chief-of-staff-kit. Once you've finished the lessons here, your agent can set it up from there.
